**Recommendation:** **dual-variant viable but borderline.** The vintage signal
at 18% is just under the 20% dual-variant threshold codified in Step 3.5d. **For
designs in the vintage/Spatula City lane, generate BOTH p4 (dark, heather grey,
navy chef-restaurant feel) and p5 (light, cream nostalgia poster look).** For
restaurant-pro aesthetic with chef-knife visuals, **p4 dominant**. The single
floral exception ("Chef Just A Girl Who Loves Cooking") stays p5.

**Key insight:** kitchen niche is the **first niche we've scored where p5
floral is functionally absent.** The buyer is mixed-gender restaurant-pro
or insider-humor — not the 50+ women floral demographic our other niches
target. **This is a different palette and aesthetic family.**

## Trademark

All proposed angles use generic terms. **Avoid every Hell's Kitchen / Ramsay,
GoT, LOTR, Marvel, Star Wars parody** — even though Hell's Kitchen has comp 19
(amazing signal), the TM risk isn't worth it.

**"Spatula City"** is a UHF (1989 Weird Al film) reference. The phrase is now
generic enough on Amazon (the data shows it selling) but if we use it we should
NOT replicate the original UHF logo or visual style — just the phrase as
generic kitchen humor.

## Recommendation

**Test kitchen carefully — 3 designs max,** focused on the niche's actual
data winners (not generic chef identity, not TM parodies):

1. **"Spatula City Since [Year]"** — vintage nostalgia, **dual-variant (p4 + p5)** — top of niche
2. **"Salted Butter Social Club"** OR **"Let him Cook"** — restaurant-insider humor, **p4 dark dominant** — under-served low-comp lane
3. **"Chef Just A Girl Who Loves Cooking" (floral)** — single floral exception, **p5 light** — broadens to the female cooking-mom buyer

**Aesthetic shift:** unlike our pet niches, kitchen rewards **typography-led
restaurant-pro aesthetic** on **darker shirts**, not floral feminine. Different
buyer demographic, different palette: deep navy / charcoal / cream / brass /
chef-white accents instead of warm farmhouse pastels.

**Honest risk:** kitchen is the niche most likely to look "samey" against
existing competitors (Hell's Kitchen, generic chef tees) because every
profitable lane is text-driven slang. Differentiation depends on hitting
fresh slang/puns the existing sellers haven't grabbed yet.
